Research Overview

My research is driven by a central question: how can we build intelligent social systems whose behaviour is not only powerful, but also scientifically interpretable, trustworthy, and causally meaningful? I study how individual cognition, social interaction, and evolving network structure jointly shape opinion change, coordination, polarization, and collective adaptation in multi-agent environments.

To address this, I work at the intersection of brain-inspired computation, social computing, trustworthy AI, and causal inference. I am particularly interested in developing cognitively grounded artificial agents and mechanism-aware artificial societies that connect micro-level learning and decision processes to macro-level social dynamics. My work draws on computational neuroscience, machine learning, and LLM-based social agents, with applications to opinion dynamics, social influence, cognitive security, and human-AI collective behaviour.

My long-term goal is to develop scientifically grounded and trustworthy artificial societies that can serve as rigorous digital testbeds for understanding, predicting, and stress-testing collective behaviour. By combining brain-inspired intelligence with principled evaluation, I aim to create AI systems that are not only capable of simulating complex social worlds, but also useful for studying interventions, governance, and the societal impact of increasingly autonomous agents.
